Warnings from the model audit

    How to read this page

    • Joint angles and end-effector poses are computed by the same model and solver the controller uses; nothing is sent to a motor.
    • Positions are shown in millimetres and rotations in degrees (roll, pitch, yaw = URDF rpy, extrinsic X-Y-Z). The API speaks SI.
    • A greyed limit means the joint is continuous with no measured soft limit; the slider range is for display only.
    • joint range: each slider spans the motor travel -- the DYNAMIXEL count range leader-follower position teleoperation drives, ±180° about the count zero, with 0° at that zero -- and not the range the CAD export declares. A measured soft limit narrows it. The solver keeps the model's own range, named in the slider's tooltip whenever it is the narrower one: drive a joint outside it and a solve stops with the configuration already violates the limits, which is what the machine would say too. Bring the joint back inside and solve again.
    • dragging a hand: the coloured ball on each TCP is the target. Grab it in the 3D view -- click, or tap and hold -- and drag: the hand follows the pointer across the plane through the target that faces the camera, the solver runs while you drag (with move on every jog ticked) and once more, with the full duration budget, when you let go. What you see is the timed motion towards the pointer, not a jump. Turn the view to reach the remaining axis, or grab with Shift held to keep x and y and slide along world Z. Only the position is dragged; the orientation stays as the boxes have it. Dragging anywhere else orbits the camera, as before.
    • bars: every joint angle and every end-effector target component has a bar. Dragging an end-effector bar re-targets the motion on each step (with move on every jog ticked) and again on release, so you can sweep a coordinate and watch the arms follow. A bar's range for x/y/z is the arm unfolded from its shoulder -- an outer bound on where the hand could be, not a claim that every point on it is reachable.
    • The grid is the floor the robot stands on: it is drawn at the bottom of the base, the geometry no joint moves. That is not the model origin -- in the Rakuda export the origin (the world triad) sits about 26 cm above the floor.
    • An inactive hand defaults to follow torso (keep joints): its six arm joints receive zero IK motion, while its world TCP can move with the torso. It still participates in collision checks. Choose hold TCP in world to request the previous weighted hold; only that mode reports a TCP hold residual. Selecting an arm captures its current pose as its target before tracking starts.
    • orientation: whether a hand can translate while holding an orientation is not a property of the wrist alone: it depends on the whole arm's configuration, its Jacobian there and the joint limits, and it changes from pose to pose. position_only asks for the position alone; pose adds the full orientation with the given weight; axis_aligned (offered when the configuration states the gripper's approach axis) keeps that axis pointing along the target's and leaves the turn about it free, which is what most grasps need. A request the arm cannot meet from where it is ends locally_stalled, limits_blocked or collision_blocked, each saying what was binding.
    • timed motion: the page never jumps to a final pose. The reference moves at most at the configured linear and angular speed and acceleration (Info shows the profile), the solver follows it one sample at a time, and the samples are played back at their own time stamps; between two samples the display interpolates the joint angles linearly, which is exact for these revolute joints. The trail in the 3D view is the TCP path the solver computed, the small white marker is the reference the hands are tracking right now, and the coloured ball is the goal. Session state (velocity history, posture reference) is kept from move to move and reset only when you edit a joint by hand, switch arm, orientation or torso mode, or press reset session.
    • status words: tracking (still moving), converged (arrived within tolerance), locally_stalled (no constraint active, the local method cannot progress from here; this does not prove the goal unreachable), limits_blocked (a joint limit is binding), collision_blocked (a collision pair is at its safety distance), stale / solver_error (the request or the QP failed; the pose is unchanged).
    • collision: self-collision avoidance is evaluated only when collision pairs are registered on the model. Loading a collision URDF registers none by itself; the End effector tab says so whenever nothing is evaluated.
    • start pose: at all-zero joint angles the arms hang fully extended, so the hands sit on the edge of the workspace and most Cartesian jogs that keep the height cannot be reached. Bend an elbow on the Joints tab first, as you would move a real arm to a home pose. Elbows bend one way only: in the Rakuda export elbow_pitch_left_dof bends positive and elbow_pitch_right_dof negative (the sliders show each range).
