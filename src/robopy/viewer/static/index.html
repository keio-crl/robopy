Warnings from the model audit

    How to read this page

    • Joint angles and end-effector poses are computed by the same model and solver the controller uses; nothing is sent to a motor.
    • Positions are shown in millimetres and rotations in degrees (roll, pitch, yaw = URDF rpy, extrinsic X-Y-Z). The API speaks SI.
    • A greyed limit means the joint is continuous with no measured soft limit; the slider range is for display only.
    • joint range: each slider spans the motor travel -- the DYNAMIXEL count range leader-follower position teleoperation drives, ±180° about the count zero, with 0° at that zero -- and not the range the CAD export declares. A measured soft limit narrows it. The solver keeps the model's own range, named in the slider's tooltip whenever it is the narrower one: drive a joint outside it and a solve stops with the configuration already violates the limits, which is what the machine would say too. Bring the joint back inside and solve again.
    • dragging a hand: the coloured ball on each TCP is the target. Grab it in the 3D view -- click, or tap and hold -- and drag: the hand follows the pointer across the plane through the target that faces the camera, the solver runs while you drag (with solve on every jog ticked) and once more, at full budget, when you let go. Turn the view to reach the remaining axis, or grab with Shift held to keep x and y and slide along world Z. Only the position is dragged; the orientation stays as the boxes have it. Dragging anywhere else orbits the camera, as before.
    • bars: every joint angle and every end-effector target component has a bar. Dragging an end-effector bar re-solves the IK on each step (with solve on every jog ticked) and again on release, so you can sweep a coordinate and watch the arms follow. A bar's range for x/y/z is the arm unfolded from its shoulder -- an outer bound on where the hand could be, not a claim that every point on it is reachable.
    • The grid is the floor the robot stands on: it is drawn at the bottom of the base, the geometry no joint moves. That is not the model origin -- in the Rakuda export the origin (the world triad) sits about 26 cm above the floor.
    • An inactive hand defaults to follow torso (keep joints): its six arm joints receive zero IK motion, while its world TCP can move with the torso. It still participates in collision checks. Choose hold TCP in world to request the previous weighted hold; only that mode reports a TCP hold residual. Re-enabling a hand captures its current pose before tracking resumes.
    • orientation: Rakuda's wrist has two axes (yaw, pitch), so a translation that keeps the hand's exact orientation is often not reachable; the solver then stops at a weighted compromise and reports stalled. soft favours position, keep weights both equally, free jogs position only.
    • start pose: at all-zero joint angles the arms hang fully extended, so the hands sit on the edge of the workspace and most Cartesian jogs that keep the height cannot be reached. Bend an elbow on the Joints tab first, as you would move a real arm to a home pose. Elbows bend one way only: in the Rakuda export elbow_pitch_left_dof bends positive and elbow_pitch_right_dof negative (the sliders show each range).
